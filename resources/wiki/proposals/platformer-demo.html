1. A platformer demo that drives the engine

Status: done · Author’s approval: proposal ☑ plan ☑

Why

Go Engine (v0.0.1) can place things in a scene and run code on them, but nothing yet makes a game feel like one: things don’t fall, collide, animate or get followed by the camera. A small platformer needs all of that, so building one tells us what the engine is missing and proves each piece works, on the desktop and on the 3DS. The author’s own godot-generic-platformer is the reference for how it should play.

What

The engine gains generic 2D pieces any game can use; the demo is a project made with the editor that uses them, with only its own behaviours as game code.

Out of scope

  • Slopes, one-way platforms, moving platforms and ladders (later, if the demo grows).
  • Enemies with their own behaviour, shooting, bounce pads.
  • Sound and music (Go Sound comes in a later change), menus, a game interface, saving.
  • 3D physics.
  • Documentation of the new pieces: v0.0.1 is not documented yet.

Decisions

  • Art: the sprites and tilesets of godot-generic-platformer, as the author allowed; the demo says where they come from.
  • Where: examples/platformer/, a project with its own go.mod, beside the engine, so each change to the engine is played with it in the same commit.
  • Scope: one world, the forest, in three short levels. Lava and snow come later, from the same pieces.

Design

Four generic pieces join the engine; the demo only adds its behaviours, scenes and art.

space ← physics ← engine ← editor

1. physics, a new package: boxes that move and collide in 2D, knowing nothing of nodes.

  • A Body has a box (its size in metres), a velocity, a gravity scale and a kind: solid (never moves), moving (falls and is pushed back by solids) or trigger (only notices what touches it).
  • World.Step(seconds) adds gravity, then moves each moving body one axis at a time, in steps no longer than half its smallest side, so a fast body cannot jump through a thin wall (R1). A body that hits a solid stops on that axis only and keeps the other, which is sliding (R2); hitting a floor sets OnFloor.
  • Overlaps of triggers are reported once, when they begin (R6).
  • Alternatives not taken: a physics library (Box2D ports, Chipmunk). They bring rotation, joints and a dependency the demo doesn’t need; boxes lined up with the axes are what platformers use, and small enough to learn from.

2. Tiles, in engine and scenefile: a node may be a tile map: a tileset picture, the size of a tile in pixels, a grid of tile numbers, and which tiles are solid (R3).

  • The tileset is cut into one picture per tile when it loads, so draw2d draws whole pictures and Go Renderer needs no change.
  • Solid tiles become solid bodies, rows of them merged into one box, so a level of a thousand tiles is a few dozen boxes.
  • The editor gains a tile tool in the 2D view: the tileset in the inspector to choose from, drag to paint, right-drag to erase.

3. Animated sprites, in engine and scenefile: a sprite may be a sheet of equal frames, with named animations (frames, frames per second, looping), and may face left (R4).

  • Frames are cut from the sheet when it loads; facing left uses a mirrored copy made once, since draw2d draws pictures upright.
  • A behaviour calls node.Play("walk") and node.FaceLeft(true).

4. Bodies, triggers, the camera and scenes, in engine:

  • A node may have a body ("body": {"kind": "moving", "size": [0.8, 0.6]} in the scene file); the engine keeps the physics world in step with the nodes every frame: behaviours update, the world steps, nodes take their bodies’ positions.
  • A behaviour with Touched(context, other *Node) hears when its node’s trigger begins touching another body (R6).
  • A camera may follow a node, with a smoothing time, kept inside the level’s bounds: the tile maps’ extent, or bounds of its own (R5). The tile maps bound the sides and the bottom; the sky above stays open, so the camera rises with a jump, unless the camera has a ceiling (a cave).
  • context.RestartScene() and context.ChangeScene(file) let a game restart a level or go to the next, in a window, on the 3DS and in the editor’s view (R6, R7).

The demo, examples/platformer/: a project like any other (its go.mod points at the engine beside it). Behaviours Player (walk, jump with Space or A, animations, facing), Coin (collected when touched), Hazard (spikes and water: restart the level) and Flag (next level); three forest levels: the first two converted from the reference’s World.tscn and World2.tscn (its third is a copy of the second), the third joining them into one wider than the screen. No behaviour of the demo reaches into the engine’s insides (R8).

Result

Built. The physics package (boxes that fall, collide, slide, and triggers that report a touch once); bodies, tile maps with merged solid rows, animated sprites that face either way, a camera that follows within bounds (the sky open above the tile maps unless the camera has a ceiling), and restarting or changing scenes, in engine; inspector sections for bodies, sheets and following cameras and a tile tool in the editor; and the demo in examples/platformer/: three forest levels (two converted from the reference’s Godot levels, the third joining them), with Player, Coin, Hazard and Flag as its only code.

Checked. Every requirement’s test passes (go test ./... here and in the demo). The demo was played by a script in the editor’s view and drawn after some seconds; on the 3DS it was built with TinyGo and played in Azahar at 60 frames a second: walking, jumping over the spikes with A, the camera rising with the jump, a restart on the spikes. That found a real bug: go-ui hands the scene a fresh copy of its input each frame, so a button was never “pressed”; the engine now keeps the game’s keys itself (engine/console.go).

Review. The reviewer’s findings were fixed: a node moved or added while playing keeps or gets its body; a body with no size takes short steps; a scene made by hand cannot restart and says so (engine.scene-not-restartable); a missing picture is tried once and costs nothing after; a restart shares the pictures already read (TakeReplacement, renamed because it acts); typed frames are checked against the sheet before a run is counted out; removing an animation forgets the texts typed for the others; the first animation can be unticked; a missing tileset says why; remove buttons have tooltips; the engine’s own code and tests name no coin, spike or player. Not changed, on purpose: DefaultGravity keeps its name (its unit, metres a second each second, is in its comment); two long editor functions (followMouse, nodeInspector) are rewritten by proposal 2’s Level task instead of split twice.

For a person. Play the demo on the console (T13): the jump’s height, the camera and the frame rate on the real machine.
