12. Sound: effects and music from Go Sound

Status: proposed · Author’s approval: proposal ☐ plan ☐

Why

Go Engine’s games are silent. A jump with no sound feels half done, and io-world’s Unity version already had a jump and a calm piece of music (jump.mp3, tranquil.mp3). Go Sound, the author’s own library, plays WAV and Ogg Vorbis on the desktop, Android, the web and the 3DS. The engine only has to use it.

What

Id Requirement Check
R1 A behaviour plays a sound by its file, once, with context.Play("assets/sounds/jump.ogg") test a played sound reaches the mixer once
R2 A scene can have music, which loops, set in its inspector, and keeps playing across a restart of the same scene tests a scene's music loops, restarting a scene keeps its music
R3 Sounds are read once, through the asset cache, and playing one allocates nothing a frame the allocation test
R4 Project shows sound files with a play button, and Play mutes and unmutes the game capture; test muting stops the game's sound
R5 The same game sounds on the desktop, Android and the 3DS heard on the emulators (Android’s reports it playing; Azahar plays it)

Out of scope

  • MP3: Go Sound reads WAV and Ogg Vorbis. io-world’s two MP3s are converted once, to Ogg.
  • Sound placed in the world (louder when near): a later change, with a listener on the camera.
  • An editor of sounds.

Design

Engine: a sounds part of the runner owns one Go Sound mixer. context.Play(file) finds the sound in the asset cache (reading it the first time) and starts a player. A scene’s music is a file and a volume, started when the scene starts. It is left alone when the next scene has the same music, so a restart does not cut it.

Scene files: Scene gains music: {file, volume}.

Editor: Project lists .wav and .ogg files with a play button. The scene’s inspector chooses its music. Play has a mute switch, and Stop silences everything.

Builds: the 3DS build links Go Sound’s 3DS output. Android needs nothing more than go-ui already asks for.

Alternative not taken: a sound component on nodes (Unity’s AudioSource). For one-off effects a call from a behaviour is shorter, and music belongs to the scene, not to a node.
