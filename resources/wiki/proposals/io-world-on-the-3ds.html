7. Pushing, riding and pulling: io-world on the 3DS

Status: done (the console check is the author’s) · Author’s approval: proposal ☑ plan ☑ (2026-10-19, “sigue”: together; the io-world repository private)

Why

io-world is a small puzzle-platformer made with Godot 3. It has one level of 100-pixel tiles and a player of three pictures (body, tail, eyes). It has jump pads, a magnet the player pushes and stands on, a platform on a rail, and a magnetic field that pulls the other way.

Made again from nothing in Go Engine and played on the 3DS, it answers two questions. Can a person make a game from scratch with the editor? And what does the engine still lack?

The engine’s physics has three kinds of body: solids that never move, moving bodies that only meet solids, and triggers. Three of io-world’s mechanics need more, and any puzzle-platformer will want the same:

  • Something to push: a box the player pushes along the floor and stands on.
  • Something to ride: a platform that moves and carries what stands on it.
  • Somewhere gravity turns: an area where bodies fall another way.

Out of scope

  • Bodies that turn, slopes, and shapes other than boxes.
  • io-world’s sounds, menus and levels beyond its first.
  • Converting Godot scenes in the editor: a one-off script, kept in the game’s own repository, as the platformer demo’s was.

Design

Physics (physics): two new kinds beside Solid, Moving and Trigger.

  • Pushable moves as Moving does. A moving body that runs into it along X moves it, by as much as it is not blocked; a chain of boxes moves together. Along Y it stops the body, which stands on it. Solids stop it as they stop any moving body.
  • Platform moves by its velocity before the others each step. Bodies whose feet rest on it move with it, and a body in its way is moved out of it, as a pushable is.
  • A trigger’s Gravity, when set, replaces the world’s for the bodies inside it during that step. A body knows which way gravity pulled it, and OnFloor means “stopped going that way”.

The order of a step: platforms, then moving and pushable bodies, then triggers.

Scene files: BodyKind gains "pushable" and "platform". Body gains gravity: [x, y], read for triggers.

Editor: Level’s body inspector offers the five kinds, and a gravity field for triggers. The body overlay draws a platform’s direction and a zone’s arrow.

io-world: a new repository, made with go-engine new. The art comes from io-world’s PNGs through Art: sprites, and a tileset of 100-pixel tiles. The level is drawn in Level, or converted once from Level1.tscn by a script in the repository. Its behaviours in Go:

  • Player: run, jump, reset.
  • Jumper: a push along its direction.
  • Rail: a platform going back and forth.
  • Field: a gravity zone the player can turn.
  • The cameras.

It is built with tinygo-3ds from Build’s Install, and sent with 3dslink.

Branches: the engine’s part on feature/physics, from feature/engine, where the other session’s work is. io-world goes in its own repository.

Result

Built.

  • Two new kinds of body, pushable and platform, and gravity zones (a trigger’s pull). They are in the physics, the scene files and Level’s inspector, each drawn its own way.
  • A body’s floor is the side its gravity pulls it to.
  • Pushing goes across that gravity, in a row of at most eight, and never round a ring.
  • A platform carries the stacks standing on it, pushes what is in its way, and stops against what it cannot move.
  • Triggers notice pushable bodies too.
  • On the 3DS, a menu of the scenes on the bottom screen (sceneMenu), or the game’s own (SetBottomScreen).

Io World has io-world’s first level, and its dev branch’s version: a button that starts a rail that waits, and a cube. A menu of levels is made of the game’s own pieces. It was sent to the author’s 3DS by FTP. It now lives in io-world’s own repository, in its IoWorldGo/ folder.

Checked. Every requirement’s tests pass: the physics’, the engine’s allocations with the new kinds, and the editor’s. Io World’s own tests cover landing, running, pads, the magnet, the rail, the field, the button and the cube. The editor was captured, and the game in Azahar, with the menu changing the level by a touch.

Not done. Bodies that turn, slopes, tiles’ own shapes and flipped tiles. io-world’s attractor tiles are partial polygons, here whole squares; flipped tiles are drawn unflipped. The original’s two fixed screens became one camera that follows Io. At 400 pixels, a whole screen’s width made the tiles 14 pixels.

What was hard, making it (for the next proposals):

  1. The editor was not where it was made. The level came from a script (tools/convert_level.py), and the behaviours from a code editor. The editor opened, played and changed it. Drawing a 90-by-37 level of 100-pixel tiles by hand, and five jump pads, would take an afternoon: there are no prefabs to place again and again.
  2. Prefabs. io-world’s jump pad, rail and button are small scenes placed many times. Here each is written out in full. Instancing a scene in another is the biggest missing piece.
  3. Sprites made of tiles. The pad and the rail are rows of a tile picture. Art cannot compose them, so the script did, into new pictures.
  4. One tileset per tile map. Godot’s tile set mixes pictures: one level became two tile maps.
  5. Touching needs overlapping. A pad’s trigger, level with the ground it is on, never notices what stands on it: it has to reach above it. Easy to get wrong, and silent.
  6. go-engine new opens the editor, so it needs a display. Making a project on a server needed project.Create from Go.
  7. Choices the original left open. The field pulled left in the level and up in its prefab, and worked in neither. The button only printed. Here the field pulls up (the inspector changes it), and the button starts the rail, as io-world’s first, Unity version did.

The original’s other scenes. io-world began in Unity, with Demoivania and MainMenu. Demoivania (December 2022) had a menu with music and lights, and a long level with levels 2 and 3 started. It had buttons, a cube, rails, magnets, liquids and another character, with about 710 brick sprites and 30 C# scripts. They went away when it moved to Godot, which only ever had Level1. They could come here too, as a later proposal.

For a person. Play both levels on the console: the controls, the rail and the field’s feel, and the menu under a thumb.
