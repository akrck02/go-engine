8. Lights, in 2D and 3D

Status: done, pending the author’s review and a check of Metal on a Mac · Author’s approval: proposal ☑ plan ☑ (2026-10-19, “Con metal”: with Metal) · go-renderer’s part, on its own feature/lights branch, is pushed only with the author’s say (its CLAUDE.md)

Why

io-world’s start screen in Unity was lit: a global light at 72 % dimmed the whole scene, the lamp cast a warm circle (#faffa0, 3.5 metres), the logo, “Press start” and the snail glowed through their own pictures, and two thin lights lined “Demo”. Made again in Go Engine (proposal 7), it is flat: the engine draws 2D with no light at all. Light is what gives a factory its gloom and a lamp its warmth; any 2D game will want it, on the desktop, on phones and on the 3DS.

Unity draws 2D lights in a pass of their own: it paints the lights into a light picture, cleared to the global light, and then multiplies the sprites by it. go-renderer’s 2D drawing only paints one thing over another, with transparency: it has no way to add light nor to multiply by it.

Out of scope

  • Shadows cast by sprites or by 3D lights, normal maps, spot lights (a later kind of the same light), and dynamic lights on the 3DS’s GPU (its fragment lighting: 3D lights are baked into vertex colours there, which suits lights that seldom move).
  • Blend modes as a general tool for programs (only what the light layer needs).

Design

go-renderer (draw2d, opengl, gles, ctr), on feature/lights:

  • draw2d: list.PointLight(x, y, inner, outer, colour) and list.SpriteLight(picture, area, colour) add lights (a colour’s alpha is its intensity), and list.Lighting(ambient) lights everything drawn before it with the lights since the last one, inside the clip it is made in; what comes after (an interface) stays unlit. (Built so instead of BeginLights/EndLights: one command marks where lighting happens, and nothing has to be closed.)
  • OpenGL and GL ES: a light target the size of the screen, at half its resolution (lights are soft), made like the backdrop’s targets and kept between frames: cleared to the ambient colour, lights drawn into it additively (a point light is one quad whose shader makes its falloff), then drawn over the frame with a multiplying blend. One more draw call per light layer.
  • 3DS (ctr): citro3d draws into a texture render target (C3D_RenderTargetCreateFromTex) at half the top screen’s resolution, 256 by 128 (sides that are powers of two), and the PICA200 adds and multiplies with its own blend modes (GPU_BLEND_ADD, GPU_DST_COLOR). With no fragment shader, a point light’s fade is read from a texture of sixteen discs, and the ambient is painted in, since citro3d’s clears run before a frame’s draws.
  • Metal: the same light target as a texture of its own, drawn with the same blends; written as the backdrop’s Metal part was. It cannot be built nor run where this is written (Linux): the author checks it on a Mac.

go-engine:

  • scenefile: a node’s light: {kind: point|sprite, colour, intensity, inner, outer}; a sprite light uses the node’s own picture, which it is drawn as instead of being shown. A scene’s ambient colour and intensity is its global light; a scene with lights and no ambient is lit by daylight. (Changed while building: no “global” kind on nodes, since the scene’s ambient is the global light, and no falloff setting: every backend fades with the same smooth curve.)
  • engine: a 2D scene draws its sprites, then its lights (in their nodes’ places and scales) into one light layer, then what goes above (the interface). Lights are found once a frame into a reused list, as the drawn nodes are.
  • editor: a Light section in Level’s inspector (kind, colour, intensity, radii), radii drawn as circles over the view when “Lights” is on, and Play drawn lit.

Io World: tools/make_start_screen.py reads the Unity menu’s Light2D components (type, colour, intensity, radii, the sprite a sprite light uses) into the start screen’s nodes.

3D (added with R8–R9). go-renderer’s scene gets a list of point lights (position, linear colour times intensity, inner and outer radius). sceneplan chooses, for each draw, the four nearest lights whose outer sphere meets its bounds (built as “nearest”, not “strongest”: the simpler rule Godot’s mobile renderer uses, and stable while nothing moves), listed by index, so every backend gets the same short list. OpenGL and GL ES add them in their lit shading next to the sun, as Lambert light times the same smoothstep fade as 2D, from a fixed array of four uniforms (within OpenGL ES 2’s uniform budget, and no light loop beyond four). The 3DS bakes them into its vertex colours with the sun, and bakes again when a light changes. Metal follows with T4b. The engine fills the list each frame from the 3D nodes that are lights, and a 3D scene’s ambient multiplies its sky light. Researched against Unity URP (Light2D, forward’s per-object light list), Godot 4 (CanvasModulate, PointLight2D, 8+8 lights per mesh on mobile), Bevy and the PICA200’s fragment lighting.

Alternatives not taken: drawing lights as translucent halos with no light picture (option A, cheap, but light cannot darken what it does not cover nor multiply colours); a light per sprite computed on the processor (does not scale, and the 3DS has a GPU for it).
