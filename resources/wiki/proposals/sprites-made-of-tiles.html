11. Sprites made of tiles, in Art

Status: proposed · Author’s approval: proposal ☐ plan ☐

Why

io-world’s jump pad and rail are rows of a tile picture. Art cannot put tiles together, so a script drew them into new pictures (proposal 7). Those pictures are copies: changing the tileset leaves them behind. An artist who draws in tiles should build bigger things from them in the editor, and see them follow the tileset.

What

Id Requirement Check
R1 Art makes a sprite from a tileset: a small grid painted with its tiles, as a tile map is test a sprite made of tiles has its tiles' pixels; capture
R2 Changing the tileset’s picture changes every sprite made from it, the next time it loads test a sprite made of tiles follows its tileset
R3 Such a sprite is used as any other: animations, lights, bodies test a sprite made of tiles plays its animations
R4 It is put together once, when it loads, and drawn as one picture the allocation test

Out of scope

  • Painting pixels: Art cuts and composes pictures; drawing them is another program’s job.
  • Turned tiles in a sprite, until proposal 10 brings them.

Design

A sprite file gains tiles, which takes the place of picture. It holds a tileset, a number of columns and rows, and a tile number per cell, as a tile map’s. The engine puts the picture together when the sprite loads, through the asset cache, and the rest of the sprite (frames, animations) cuts it as if it were a file.

Art gets New sprite from tiles: the tileset’s palette on the right, a grid in the middle to paint, and the usual animation strip under it. Its grid’s size is the sprite’s frames: a pad of three tiles in two frames is three columns by two rows.

Alternative not taken: saving the composed picture as a PNG. It is simpler to load, but it stops following the tileset (R2), which is the reason for this change.
