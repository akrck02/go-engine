🧱 A 2D level with tiles

Paint a level with a tileset, and let its solid tiles be the ground. For a first walk through it, see level 3 of the quest.

The shortest way

  1. In Project, choose a picture of tiles and press Make it a sprite or a tileset, then A tileset. Set Pixels a tile, a side (16, 32…) and save. The tileset is a file beside the picture, name.tileset.json, that every scene shares.
  2. Still in Art, click the tiles bodies should stand on: they turn blue, solid.
  3. In Level, press +, Tile map, and choose the tileset in its inspector. Set how many cells across and down it has.
  4. Choose a tile in the palette under the view, and paint: B paints, E erases, G fills, V goes back to choosing nodes.

Set the scene’s pixels per metre to the tile’s size, so a tile is a metre.

How it behaves
Where The map’s top-left corner is its node’s position; a cell is a tile’s pixels at the scene’s pixels per metre
Solid tiles Each run of solid tiles side by side becomes one solid box while the game plays: the ground, walls and ceilings
Depth The map is drawn at its node’s draw order, like any 2D node
Several maps A scene can have many: a background one with no solid tiles, a foreground one to stand on

Variations

  • A bare picture as tiles. A tile map can name a picture instead of a tileset. Its inspector then asks for the tile size, and the palette has Bodies stand on tile … to choose solid ones. A tileset is better as soon as two scenes share the tiles.
  • Bigger or smaller maps. Changing the cells across and down keeps what is painted from the top-left corner, and empties the rest.
  • A camera inside the level. A camera that follows keeps its view inside the tile maps: see A camera that follows.
