🎮 Actions and controls

This guide is for whoever makes a game that plays on several devices. At the end you will read what the player does by name, give it buttons on each device in the editor, and design the sticks and buttons a phone shows.

Read actions, not keys

A game that reads Space works on a keyboard and nowhere else. A game that reads actions works anywhere: it asks for jump, and each device says which of its buttons jump.

func (hero *Hero) Update(context *engine.Context) {
	if context.Input.Action("jump").Pressed {
		hero.Jump()
	}
	hero.Turn(context.Input.Action("turn").Value) // from -1 to 1
}

An action presses (Down, Pressed, Released) or moves an axis (Value, from -1 to 1).

Give them buttons in the editor

Open the Controls workspace. The actions are cards on the left, each in its own colour; the device is drawn in the middle, chosen in its tabs: Gamepad, Keyboard and mouse, Nintendo 3DS and Mobile. A pad and a keyboard play the same game on a computer and in a browser, so what you give them holds for both.

  • Click a control of the device: a panel opens beside it. Pick an action, or type a new name and create it there. A button presses; a stick has two axes, across and up and down, each with its own action.
  • The cards say which controls do each action; click one to see its controls stand out. The pencil renames it, the next icon switches it between pressing and an axis.
  • Every change is kept in engine.json at once. Ctrl+Z undoes the last one.

Design a phone’s controls

A phone has no buttons of its own, so the game draws them. Until you design some, it shows the engine’s four: the arrows, B and A. In Mobile → Layout:

  1. Design the game’s own starts from a stick and two buttons.
  2. Stick and Button add more; drag each where the thumbs rest.
  3. With one chosen, rename it, make it smaller or bigger, or remove it.

Back in Actions, the controls on the screen get their actions like any other. Places and sizes are shares of the screen, so the layout fits every phone.
